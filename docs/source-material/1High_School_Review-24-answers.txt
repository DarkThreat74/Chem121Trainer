17. a. Mole is a quantity—6.02 x 1023. It is similar to dozen—12. Molarity is a concentration
(moles/Liter)—depending on the volume, you may have more or less than a mole.
b. The amount of something is the quantity of that thing. If you have 1.0 mole NaCl, then you
have 6.02 x 1023 molecules of NaCl. Concentration is the quantity of a thing per volume. If
you have 1.0 molar NaCl, then the actual amount of NaCl would depend on the volume. If
you have a liter of 1.0 molar NaCl, then you would have 1.0 mole of NaCl. But if you only
have 0.5 liter of 1.0 molar NaCl, then you would only have 0.5 mole of NaCl. The
concentration of a solution does not tell you how much of that molecule you have unless you
also know the volume of your solution.

a. The solute adds volume to the solution so you cannot simply add the final volume of solvent.
For example, imagine you want to make a 0.10 g/mL solution of glucose. If you add 10 g
glucose to 100 mL water, your final volume of solution is now more than 100 mL (closer to
108 mL or so) and therefore your concentration would be slightly less than 0.10 g/mL
glucose. While masses are additive, volumes are not. Therefore, you must allow the solute to
dissolve because a dissolved solute takes up a different volume than the solid or pure liquid.
The volume might be more or less depending on the properties of both the solute and solvent.

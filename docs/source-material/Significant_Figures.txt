Significant Figures
Why do we care about significant figures?
Measurement, by its nature, has uncertainty. Did you measure to the nearest L? mL? L? The way we
let people know how certain we are in our measurement is by significant figures. If I use a balance to
find the mass of an object and the measurement is 2.000 g, that tells me that I have uncertainty in the
mg place; I have measured to the nearest mg (0.001 g). If a balance gives a value of 2 g, then I only have
certainty to the nearest gram.
Rules for determining significant figures in a reported number
Defined conversions
Some conversions are exact because they are defined quantities. For example, there are exactly 1000
mg in exactly 1 g; 1 inch ≡ 2.54 cm. Because it is exact, it would have an infinite number of significant
figures.
Basic Rules for determining significant figures in a measured quantity (see also Chapter 1 of your text)
1. All non-zero numbers are significant.
• Example: 12.3 has 3 significant figures
2. All zeros between non-zero numbers are significant (your book calls these “captive” zeros). All
zeros between significant zero digits are also significant.
• Example: 12.03 has 4 significant figures
3. All zeros to the left of the first non-zero number are insignificant (your book calls these
“leading” zeros). These are simply place-holding zeros—they tell us where the decimal point is
located.
• Example: 0.0123 has 3 significant figures
4. All zeros to the right of a non-zero number AND to the right of the decimal point are significant
(your book calls these “trailing” zeros). These zeros do not need to be reported unless they are
significant—so we know they are significant if they are reported.
• Example: 123.0 and 12.30 both have 4 significant figures
5. Zeros that are the last reported digits AND are to the right of a non-zero number AND before the
decimal point (your book calls these “trailing” zeros) are ambiguous. They might be significant
but they might not. Therefore, we have to count them as insignificant. If they are indeed
significant, you need to use scientific notation to denote their significance. Otherwise, they are
seen as simple place-holding zeros.
• Examples: 123,000 has 3 significant figures but 1.23000 x 105 has 6 significant figures
